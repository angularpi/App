Field Report · Cycle 2317.4

Equus astralis and the Anomalous Biosphere of Asteroid XQ-1189

Filed by the Joint Xenobiological Survey, Kessler–Ptolemy Expedition VII (“Operation Glitterhoof”)

I.The Body

Asteroid XQ-1189 (“Xiquin” in the informal nomenclature adopted by Survey personnel after a labeling error nobody has bothered to correct since 2298) is a C-type rocky body measuring 41.6 kilometers along its major axis, tumbling through a wildly eccentric 214-day orbit that carries it from a perihelion just inside Venus’s orbit out to an aphelion brushing past Earth’s own path.

This orbital indecision — informally termed “commuting” by Survey astronomers — means XQ-1189 experiences two distinct “seasons” per cycle: Venusward Scorch and Terraward Drizzle, neither of which should, by any conventional model, support a breathable atmosphere.

And yet it does.

Spectrographic analysis credits this to what the Survey’s geophysics division has, after seventeen peer-reviewed arguments and one thrown clipboard, agreed to call Residual Thaumo-Cryogenic Outgassing — the theory that XQ-1189’s core contains a compacted lattice of pre-Cambrian cometary ice infused with what can only be described as “old magic,” left over from an unspecified but clearly eventful period in the asteroid’s geological history. This ice sublimates at a slow, magically regulated rate, producing a thin but stable atmospheric shell 1.3 kilometers thick, held in place not by gravity (XQ-1189’s surface gravity is a modest 0.008g) but by what Lead Geomancer Dr. Petra Vinh-Okonkwo-Lindqvist calls “the asteroid’s very strong personal opinion that it should have air.”

Surface features include the Vitreous Highlands (a mountain range composed entirely of fused impact glass), the Hoofprint Sea (a dry basin of pulverized regolith so fine it behaves like liquid under low gravity, and which — coincidentally — is shaped like a 30-kilometer-wide hoofprint), and the Long Furrow, a 90-kilometer scar of disputed origin that the Survey’s official position describes as “definitely a meteor strike” and everyone else calls “the running track.”

II.Physiology of Equus astralis

The Xiquin unicorn stands roughly 1.4 meters at the shoulder, has a bone density 60% lower than its terrestrial equine cousins, and possesses lungs that have essentially given up on the whole endeavor. Rather than processing atmospheric oxygen through respiration in the conventional sense, E. astralis draws ambient thaumo-cryogenic particulate directly through its horn, which functions as a combination air-filtration organ, antenna, and — Survey biologists insist, entirely without evidence — “emotional resonance transmitter.”

This particulate is metabolized in a specialized organ cluster behind the sternum called the glim-bladder, which converts it into usable bioenergy and, as a waste byproduct, mane luminescence. This glow is measured in Sparkle-Candela (Sp-cd), a unit developed specifically for this species after the Survey’s standard photometric equipment returned “error: too much” on three separate occasions.

This event remains the highest verified Sp-cd reading on record and has since been used, unofficially, as the calibration ceiling for all subsequent field instruments — a setting engineers refer to simply as “Gary.”

Locomotion in XQ-1189’s near-nonexistent gravity is achieved not through galloping in the traditional sense but through a controlled series of hoof-plants and glide-bounds, each covering 30 to 50 meters and lasting an average of 6.8 seconds — a gait the Survey has clinically classified as “showing off.”

III.Diet, Water, and Other Logistical Inconveniences

In near-vacuum conditions with no arable soil, E. astralis subsists primarily on stellar lichen (Lichenus improbabilis), a photosynthetic — or, more accurately, “starlight-synthetic” — organism that grows directly on exposed rock and derives its biomass from solar wind particles, cosmic ray bombardment, and, per one unconfirmed field note, “spite.” Herds supplement this diet by grazing directly on comet-tail sublimate, drifting curtains of ice crystal and dust shed by passing comets, which unicorns consume mid-glide by tossing their heads through the particulate cloud — a behavior the Survey has charmingly, if unscientifically, logged as “snacking.”

Water is obtained internally: the glim-bladder, in addition to its luminescent duties, moonlights as a hygroscopic condenser, drawing trace water molecules out of the thin atmosphere at a rate of approximately 1.1 liters per unicorn per week. This is, all parties agree, nowhere near enough water to sustain a large mammal, and the Survey’s official stance on this discrepancy is “we are choosing not to think about it too hard.”

IV.Social Structure and the Solar Wind Migration

Xiquin unicorns are herd animals, organizing into matrilineal groups of 8 to 14 individuals called drifts, each led by a senior mare known as the Wind-Reader, whose enlarged horn is believed to detect subtle shifts in solar wind density up to 40,000 kilometers out.

Twice per orbital cycle — during the transition between Venusward Scorch and Terraward Drizzle — drifts convene into a super-herd of up to 300 individuals for what Survey xenoethologists term the Solar Wind Migration, a slow, luminous procession along the asteroid’s dawn terminator, timed to coincide with peak particulate density. Observed from orbit, a migrating super-herd resembles, in the words of Expedition photographer Insu Marchetti-Yaw, “a river made entirely of Christmas.”

This migration serves a dual purpose: access to fresher grazing along the solar-wind-facing hemisphere, and — per three independent field logs, all suspiciously giddy in tone — courtship.
